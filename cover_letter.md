# Cover Letter

Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Cognitive state transitions and stage-graded stroke associations in older Chinese adults: a multi-state analysis of CHARLS 2018–2023," for consideration at BMC Geriatrics.

Cognitive assessment is a routine part of geriatric practice, and a single abnormal result increasingly triggers clinical and research action. Our findings speak directly to how such results should be interpreted in older adults, and we believe they fit the journal's clinical and health-services readership in three ways:

1. A single CIND screen is unstable — confirmation should precede action. In a national cohort of 9,679 Chinese adults aged ≥ 60 years followed with a multi-state Markov model (normal cognition ↔ CIND ↔ dementia → death), reversion from CIND to normal cognition was the dominant non-fatal transition (0.398 per person-year; 5-year probability 47%), far exceeding progression to dementia (0.086 per year). For geriatric practice and screening programmes, this means a one-off impaired classification should be confirmed before labelling, referral, or trial enrolment. Robustness is supported by five pre-specified sensitivity analyses: alternative IQCODE dementia cut-offs, a stricter CIND definition, multiple imputation of missing covariates, continuous-time covariate models, and a cross-wave language-domain equivalence check (Cohen's κ = 0.933).

2. Transition-resolved associations of stroke, relevant to post-stroke cognitive care. Rather than a single pooled risk estimate, we show that prevalent stroke is associated specifically with progression to dementia (RRR ≈ 2.0–2.4) and with death from CIND (RRR 1.95), while incident stroke is associated with a more than six-fold higher rate of interval transition from normal cognition to dementia (RRR 6.47, 95% CI 3.59–11.67). This stage-graded pattern, presented as descriptive given non-significant formal between-transition tests, argues for framing stroke prevention and post-stroke follow-up as slowing progression along the cognitive continuum — a message of particular weight in the world's largest ageing and highest-stroke-burden population.

3. These analyses are enabled by the first use of the newly released CHARLS wave 6 (2021–2023) for cognitive-state research. All prior algorithm-based CHARLS dementia studies end at 2018; wave 6 roughly doubles the available follow-up window and adds a post-COVID assessment. Mortality is modelled as a competing risk throughout, with a thirteen-fold gradient of death intensity from normal cognition to dementia.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and follows a pre-specified analysis plan with dated amendments. All authors have approved the manuscript for submission. CHARLS data are publicly available upon registration; the complete analysis code is publicly available at https://github.com/ronaldchen1985/charls-cognitive-transitions (release v1.0.0) and will be archived in a public repository with a DOI (ScienceDB, https://www.scidb.cn, or Zenodo) before publication.
