# Cover Letter

Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Cognitive state transitions and stage-graded stroke associations in older Chinese adults: a multi-state analysis of CHARLS 2018–2023," for consideration at Alzheimer's Research & Therapy.

This study makes three contributions that we believe fit the journal's scope:

1. First use of the newly released CHARLS wave 6 (2021–2023) for algorithmic dementia research. All prior algorithm-based CHARLS dementia studies end at 2018. Wave 6 roughly doubles the available follow-up window and adds a post-COVID assessment; our analysis is, to our knowledge, the first to exploit it for cognitive-state classification.

2. A complete multi-state quantification in a Chinese national cohort. Using a continuous-time Markov model (normal cognition ↔ CIND ↔ dementia → death) in 9,679 adults aged ≥ 60 years, we report transition intensities, sojourn times, and 5-year transition probabilities across the full cognitive continuum, including reversion from CIND to normal cognition (5-year probability 47%) — a parameter that, to our knowledge, has not previously been estimated in CHARLS under an algorithmic, informant-anchored classification, and that has direct implications for trial design and screening programmes. Robustness is supported by five pre-specified sensitivity analyses: alternative IQCODE dementia cut-offs, a stricter CIND definition, multiple imputation of missing covariates, continuous-time covariate models, and a cross-wave language-domain equivalence check (Cohen's κ = 0.933).

3. Transition-resolved associations of stroke. Rather than a single pooled risk estimate, we show that prevalent stroke is associated specifically with progression to dementia (RRR ≈ 2.0–2.4) and with death from CIND (RRR 1.95), while incident stroke is associated with a more than six-fold higher rate of interval transition from normal cognition to dementia (RRR 6.47, 95% CI 3.59–11.67; ascertainable among wave-6 survivors). We report this stage-graded pattern together with formal between-transition coefficient tests, which were underpowered and non-significant, and we therefore present the pattern as descriptive. Depressive symptoms show a parallel progression-specific pattern. These findings are relevant to vascular dementia prevention in the world's largest ageing and highest-stroke-burden population.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and follows a pre-specified analysis plan with dated amendments. CHARLS data are publicly available upon registration; the complete analysis code is publicly available at https://github.com/ronaldchen1985/charls-cognitive-transitions and will be archived on Zenodo with a DOI before publication.
